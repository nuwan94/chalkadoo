## An equation is a balance

Every equation is a promise: *the left side weighs exactly the same as the right side.*

Picture it as a scale. Each blue box is a mystery number we call $x$. Each yellow counter weighs 1.

$$
x + 3 = 7
$$

One mystery box and three counters on the left. Seven counters on the right. And the scale is perfectly level.

<DooSays>Technically, this is a *platform* scale, not a hanging one. I'll allow it. This once.</DooSays>

</Step>

<Step>

## Your turn: free the x

Here's $x + 3 = 7$ on the scale. Lift things off until $x$ is alone, **without** letting the scale tip.

<BalanceScale left={[1, 3]} right={[0, 7]} />

</Step>

<Step>

## The golden rule

To keep it level, you took 3 off **both** sides. That's the whole trick:

> Whatever you do to one side, do to the other.

$$
x + 3 - 3 = 7 - 3 \quad\Rightarrow\quad x = 4
$$

<MultipleChoice
  prompt="Which move keeps $x + 5 = 9$ balanced and gets $x$ closer to being alone?"
  options={[
    "Subtract 5 from the left side only",
    "Subtract 5 from both sides",
    "Add 5 to both sides",
    "Subtract 9 from both sides",
  ]}
  answer={1}
  hint="You want to cancel the $+5$. Whatever happens on the left has to happen on the right too."
  explanation="$x + 5 - 5 = 9 - 5$, so $x = 4$. Both sides lose 5 and the scale stays level."
/>

</Step>

<Step>

## Solve it without the scale

No blocks this time. Just you and the golden rule.

<NumericAnswer
  prompt="Solve $x + 8 = 15$."
  label="$x =$"
  answer={7}
  hint="Take 8 off both sides."
  explanation="$x + 8 - 8 = 15 - 8$, so $x = 7$. Check: $7 + 8 = 15$ ✓"
/>

<NumericAnswer
  prompt="Now a sneaky one: solve $x - 4 = 9$."
  label="$x =$"
  answer={13}
  hint="Here $x$ has had 4 taken away. Undo that by adding 4 to both sides."
  explanation="$x - 4 + 4 = 9 + 4$, so $x = 13$. Undo a subtraction with an addition."
/>

</Step>

What about $2x + 1 = 7$? Two mystery boxes, and they weigh the same.

Lift off the counters first. Then split both sides into equal groups.

<Step>

## Undo things in reverse

To build $2x + 1$ from $x$, you **doubled** it and **then added** 1. To undo it, go in reverse, like taking off your shoes before your socks.

<OrderSteps
  prompt="Put the steps for solving $3x + 2 = 14$ in order."
  items={[
    "Start: $3x + 2 = 14$",
    "Subtract 2 from both sides: $3x = 12$",
    "Divide both sides by 3: $x = 4$",
    "Check: $3 \\times 4 + 2 = 14$ ✓",
  ]}
  hint="Deal with the $+2$ before you divide."
  explanation="Undo the last thing that was done to $x$ first. Then check your answer by putting it back into the equation."
/>

</Step>

<Step>

## Try one with a minus

<NumericAnswer
  prompt="Solve $5x - 3 = 22$."
  label="$x =$"
  answer={5}
  hint="First add 3 to both sides. Then divide both sides by 5."
  explanation="$5x - 3 + 3 = 22 + 3$ gives $5x = 25$. Then $x = 25 \div 5 = 5$."
/>

<DooSays mood="thinking">You can't put a "minus 3" on a real scale. That's what the next lesson is for. *Balloons* are involved.</DooSays>

<Step>

## Genius level: no numbers at all

Mathematicians like to solve *every* problem at once. Suppose $x + a = b$, where $a$ and $b$ could be any numbers at all.

<ExpressionAnswer
  prompt="Make $x$ the subject of $x + a = b$. In other words, write $x$ using only $a$ and $b$."
  label="$x =$"
  answer="b - a"
  variables={["a", "b"]}
  hint="Same move as always: take $a$ off both sides."
  explanation="$x + a - a = b - a$, so $x = b - a$. Put in $a = 3$ and $b = 7$ and you get our very first answer, $x = 4$."
/>

<DooSays>Congratulations, you just wrote a *formula*. I wrote my first one at age four. It was about the optimal ratio of crackers to cheese.</DooSays>

</Step>

<Step>

## What you've learned

- An equation is a balance: both sides weigh the same.
- **The golden rule:** whatever you do to one side, do to the other.
- Undo operations in reverse: deal with adding and subtracting first, then dividing.
- Always check by putting your answer back into the equation.
